What should a nonspouse beneficiary check before moving an inherited traditional IRA?

Published October 15, 2026

Ask the current and receiving institutions to explain the proposed movement, the beneficiary account title and which instructions or documents they need. Keep their written answers before you authorize anything. A nonspouse inherited traditional IRA is not your own IRA: the IRS distinguishes a permitted trustee-to-trustee transfer from a rollover into or out of that inherited account.

This US guide is a pre-transfer question checklist for an individual who inherited a traditional IRA from someone other than a deceased spouse. It does not cover a surviving spouse's choices, inherited workplace plans, Roth IRAs, trusts or estates. It does not recommend moving the account, select an institution or provide individualized transfer instructions. Your beneficiary distribution obligations are a separate question; this article does not recap or calculate them.

Key takeaways

  • Verify that the account is an inherited traditional IRA and that the nonspouse scope fits your situation.
  • Ask the institutions to distinguish the proposed trustee-to-trustee movement from a payment to you or a rollover.
  • Check how the receiving inherited IRA will be titled and maintained, rather than relying on the word transfer alone.
  • Keep document requests, written responses and open questions identifiable before giving instructions.
  • Treat contribution-basis records and distribution obligations as separate reviews, not as automatically resolved by a move.

Why is the movement type a separate question?

Publication 590-B says that if you inherit a traditional IRA from someone other than your deceased spouse, you cannot treat it as your own, make contributions to it or roll amounts into or out of it. It permits a trustee-to-trustee transfer when the receiving IRA is set up and maintained in the deceased owner's name for your benefit as beneficiary. [1]

That distinction is the reason for this checklist. It is not a recommendation to transfer, nor a statement that any form labelled transfer meets the condition. Ask both institutions how their proposed process matches the inherited IRA's status. Do not use instructions for moving your own IRA as if they necessarily apply to an inherited account.

The broader inheritance guide covers identifying the asset and beneficiary questions. This article begins only after you have identified a nonspouse inherited traditional IRA and are considering a change of institution. If the account type or beneficiary status is uncertain, keep that as the first unresolved question.

What should I ask the current institution?

Ask it to identify the account type and recorded beneficiary title through its approved process. Ask what it requires to discuss a proposed trustee-to-trustee transfer and which documents support your authority to give instructions. A relationship to the person who died is not a substitute for the institution confirming the account and its process.

Ask whether the movement described in its paperwork sends funds directly between institutions or involves a distribution to you. Do not treat a check, a payment request and an institution-to-institution transfer as interchangeable without explanation. This guide does not tell you how to complete a form or endorse a particular route.

Keep the answer, the document version and the contact reference. If the response uses a term you do not understand, ask for the distinction before authorizing the request. A phone note that says "they can move it" leaves too much unclear.

What should I ask the receiving institution?

Ask how it will establish and maintain the inherited traditional IRA, including how the deceased owner and beneficiary are identified in the title. The IRS's stated condition concerns the deceased owner's name and your benefit as beneficiary. [1] Do not invent a universal account-title format from that general description; the institution should explain its actual documentation.

Ask which records it needs before it can review the request and which questions remain with the current institution. Keep an account-opening acknowledgement separate from confirmation of how a transfer would be handled. The existence of a new account does not establish that the movement has occurred or that every record has been received.

Request a clear explanation of any fees, restrictions or items that cannot be moved through the proposed process. Record the actual answer rather than assigning a standard price, timing or result. This checklist contains no universal transfer deadline or claim that all holdings can move unchanged.

Which answers should be compared before instructions?

Compare the two institutions' descriptions of the account, the proposed movement and the documents each needs. If one describes a beneficiary transfer and the other describes a personal IRA rollover, preserve that difference and ask for clarification. Do not choose the more convenient wording to make the forms appear consistent.

Keep the scope narrow. You are not scoring institutions, selecting investments or comparing predicted returns. You are checking whether the proposed paperwork and explanations address the inherited-account question you actually asked.

A useful private note can contain the institution contacted, the exact question, the dated written response, the relevant form or title reference and the item still unresolved. This is an original preparation aid, not an IRS-required form or evidence that a transfer is suitable for you.

Which questions need a separate review?

A movement question does not settle an inherited IRA's distribution requirements. Ask the appropriate institution and tax professional how an intended move relates to obligations in your case, without assuming that changing institutions resets or removes them. This article deliberately leaves beneficiary deadlines and withdrawal calculations to the broader source-backed review.

Contribution basis is also separate. If the person who died may have made nondeductible traditional IRA contributions, preserve that evidence for a tax professional. A separate records review concerns Forms 8606 and supporting contribution and distribution documents. Keep it apart from transfer status; do not substitute the account's market value for contribution history.

Ask who is responsible for answering each question. An institution can explain its forms and account process; a tax professional may need to assess the tax implications. Do not assume that an administrative confirmation supplies an individualized tax conclusion.

What if an answer or record is missing?

Name the missing item and the party being asked for it. Keep a request separate from receipt and review. If a form arrives but does not answer the question about account title or movement type, the question remains open.

For a fictional example, a beneficiary has a receiving-account acknowledgement but no written explanation of whether the proposed movement is trustee-to-trustee. The note records that narrow gap and the question sent to the institutions. It does not label the movement approved, tax-free or completed.

This guide provides no instruction to proceed while the gap remains. Use the institutions' actual process and seek qualified review when needed. Preserve sensitive records privately; a public inquiry requires no account numbers or copies of the deceased person's tax documents.

The bottom line

Before giving instructions, ask how the institutions will handle the nonspouse inherited traditional IRA, how the receiving account will be titled and what documents support the process. Keep their answers and any disagreement visible. The checklist helps you prepare a narrower conversation; it does not authorize a transfer, resolve tax treatment or replace distribution and contribution-basis reviews.

Common questions

Can a nonspouse beneficiary treat an inherited traditional IRA as their own?

Publication 590-B says no for the nonspouse situation described here. A surviving spouse's choices are outside this guide's scope.

Is a rollover the same as a trustee-to-trustee transfer here?

No. The IRS distinguishes them for a nonspouse inherited traditional IRA. Ask the institutions to explain which process their proposed paperwork describes.

Does an account-opening acknowledgement prove the transfer is ready?

No. Keep opening, document review, movement instructions and received records separately identified. An acknowledgement does not answer every question.

Does this checklist explain my beneficiary withdrawal deadlines?

No. Distribution obligations need their own review of the beneficiary facts and applicable rules. Do not infer that a change of institution resolves them.

Can I use a market-value statement to establish nondeductible contribution basis?

It does not establish that history by itself. Preserve the relevant contribution and tax records for a tax professional; this guide does not calculate basis.

Sources

[1] IRS, Publication 590-B (2025), What if You Inherit an IRA?, Inherited from someone other than spouse
