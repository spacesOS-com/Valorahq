This page is a collection of retirement calculators, not a single calculator with one set of inputs. Choose a tool by the question you want to explore: accumulating a balance, estimating a withdrawal amount, estimating a fixed-withdrawal duration or reviewing a particular account rule. Keep each tool's assumptions beside its result.

A collection cannot turn several estimates into a retirement plan automatically. The tools have different inputs, timing and exclusions. An output from one tool does not verify that another tool's assumptions fit your household.

Key takeaways

  • Start with the question, then select the tool.
  • Growth, withdrawal amount and withdrawal duration are different models.
  • Save inputs and limits rather than combining unlabeled outputs.
  • Review account rules separately from hypothetical growth.

How to choose the right tool

For a hypothetical accumulation question, for example, open the retirement savings growth calculator. Its companion explains a starting balance, regular additions, a period and a constant assumed return. It does not read your actual account statement, determine affordability or promise an ending balance.

For a question about how many years fixed annual withdrawals could last under a constant assumed return, open how long your savings will last. Its finite duration or Indefinitely branch is a model result, not a guarantee or a monthly bill calendar.

If your question is an amount derived from a chosen withdrawal-rate assumption, the withdrawal amount calculator is a different tool. Read its inputs and caveat. The word in a URL does not establish that a withdrawal is safe for you, and this hub does not recommend a rate.

For an account-rule question, use the relevant account tool only with its stated year, scope and source limits. For example, a contribution-limit or required-distribution tool is not a growth forecast. Check the applicable rule and personal facts separately rather than using a modeled balance as evidence of eligibility.

A worked example of choosing, not a new hub calculation

Consider a fictional reader with two questions: "What could a starting balance plus monthly additions look like under fixed assumptions?" and "How long could a fixed annual withdrawal last?" The first question belongs in the savings-growth tool. The second belongs in the savings-duration tool. This hub performs neither calculation itself.

The reader saves a separate scenario record for each tool. In the growth record, they note additions and the modeled period. In the duration record, they note the annual withdrawal and the constant-return assumption. They do not label the first tool's ending balance as guaranteed money or the second tool's duration as an exact depletion date.

If the question changes to actual bill timing or account permissions, neither calculation settles it. That is the worked example's purpose: choosing the tool also identifies the question that remains outside it. No imaginary hub inputs or combined retirement score are introduced.

Talk it through

Understand why input timing matters

The SEC's Investor.gov compound interest calculator distinguishes an initial investment, monthly contribution, interest-rate assumptions and compounding frequency. Those are separate choices. A result from a tool with one timing convention should not be treated as interchangeable with a tool using another.

Valora's compound-interest guide explains its monthly steps and month-end additions. The savings-duration guide explains a fixed annual model. Before comparing results, ask whether the rates, additions, withdrawals and periods mean the same thing. Do not silently substitute APY for a nominal rate or a monthly spending figure for an annual withdrawal.

Keep a private tool-selection worksheet

Record six fields: the question; selected tool and date; inputs and units; timing and rate assumptions; displayed result; and unanswered decision or excluded factor. Keep one row per tool. The original blank CSV is a preparation aid, not a retirement recommendation.

Write down whether an input came from a confirmed record or a hypothetical assumption. A tool's calculation does not verify a salary, spending estimate, account rule or return. If a result appears unexpected, keep the exact inputs and review the tool's guide rather than forcing a different question into it.

Related consumer guidance

The career-break guide separates document, coverage and account questions from spending assumptions. The household income change guide distinguishes payment evidence, expectations and bill timing. Those records can explain what you need reviewed without turning a calculator into a whole-household model.

The market-drop-before-retirement article addresses changing market conditions. A constant-return tool does not reproduce a path of different annual gains and losses. Keep that limit visible when deciding what review you want.

Bring the unresolved question to a professional

A useful request states which tool you used, its inputs, the assumption you want checked and the work you want delivered. Ask for written scope, costs, exclusions and responsibility for follow-up. Do not send confidential financial documents through a public inquiry.

Retirement calculators questions

Is this page one interactive calculator?

No. It is a hub linking separate retirement tools. Each tool has its own inputs, assumptions and output.

Which tool addresses hypothetical accumulation?

The retirement savings growth tool addresses a starting balance and contributions under fixed assumptions. Read its own timing and exclusions.

Which tool estimates a fixed-withdrawal duration?

How long your savings will last uses savings, a fixed annual withdrawal and a constant assumed annual return. Its output is not a guarantee.

Can I combine the tools into an automatic retirement plan?

No. Different inputs and exclusions do not become a complete plan merely by putting the outputs together.

Does a modeled balance establish an account-rule permission?

No. Account rules, eligibility and applicable-year facts need a separate check from hypothetical growth.

What should I save with a result?

Save the question, tool and date, inputs and units, timing and rate assumptions, displayed result and unanswered decision or excluded factor.
