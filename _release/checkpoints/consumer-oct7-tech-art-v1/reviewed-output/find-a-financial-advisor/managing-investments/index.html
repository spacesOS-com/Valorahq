Managing investments: compare the work, authority and full cost

Choose an investment-management engagement by its written services, trading authority, custody arrangements, costs and review process. A portfolio proposal alone does not explain what the professional will do, what remains your responsibility or how you can check the relationship.

Key takeaways

  • Compare the written work and exclusions before judging a sample portfolio or fee label.
  • Verify advice, trading authority and custody as separate responsibilities rather than assuming one label explains them all.
  • Ask for all-in costs and a clear monitoring and reporting process using the actual proposed documents.
  • Check registration and background independently, then assess relevant experience, conflicts and fit without treating registration as an endorsement.

Write the assignment before comparing portfolios

Start with the decisions and work you want help with, then ask how the proposed engagement covers them. “Manage my money” leaves too much room for different assumptions about planning, implementation and monitoring.

You may want recommendations you implement yourself, an ongoing relationship in which someone trades within agreed authority, or a planning project before deciding on management. These are scope questions, not a recommendation that one arrangement is best. Ask what is actually offered and what is excluded. A service described on a website may not be the service proposed in your agreement.

Investor.gov's investment-adviser guidance explains that advisers may provide investment advice, monitoring and financial planning, while the services and fees ultimately depend on the negotiated contract. Request that contract, relevant disclosures and a plain-language description of the work.

Make your initial question specific: which accounts and goals need review, what you want monitored and which decisions you expect to retain. Ask who performs the work, how you contact them and what happens if that person changes. A recognizable firm name does not define the responsibilities of the person you meet.

Separate advice, trading authority and custody

Ask who recommends investments, who can authorize trades and who holds the assets. Those are different responsibilities and should not be bundled into a vague promise to “take care of everything.”

For an advice-only arrangement, ask which actions you must take after receiving recommendations and whether implementation support is included. For a discretionary arrangement, ask what the adviser may do without asking for approval on each trade, what restrictions you can set and how those instructions are recorded. For any other arrangement, have the professional explain the permissions in the actual documents rather than relying on a label.

Investor.gov's Form CRS guidance describes disclosures about monitoring, investment authority, limitations and account requirements. Use the applicable relationship summary alongside the agreement and Form ADV brochure. Do not assume a summary replaces the contract or covers every service discussed in a meeting.

Ask separately about withdrawing money, deducting fees and accessing account information. Trading permission is not the same question as authority to move assets out of the account. Do not give account passwords or authentication codes to someone because they say management requires it. Establish the legitimate account-access process with the relevant firm.

Understand allocation and diversification without adopting a model

Ask how the adviser connects investment decisions to your goals, time horizon and capacity to bear losses. A generic stock-and-bond split is not proof that your household has been assessed.

Investor.gov explains asset allocation as dividing investments among categories, while diversification spreads money among investments to reduce risk. Allocation and diversification are related, but choosing several holdings does not automatically create a diversified portfolio. Discuss the underlying exposures, not just the number of account lines.

Ask which accounts the analysis includes and which it leaves out. Existing employer shares, business ownership or investments held elsewhere may affect the household question even if the adviser does not manage them. Do not assume that every outside asset must move to the firm to be acknowledged in planning; ask what review is included.

Diversification cannot promise profit or eliminate all market losses. Ask how the professional discusses downside, changing cash needs and limits of the plan. This guide supplies no age-based formula, recommended allocation or market forecast. A useful explanation should make assumptions visible and tell you what information could change the approach.

Compare scope and costs with a written worksheet

Compare candidates using the same service questions and written evidence, not just the advertised fee. Record what is included, what is excluded and what remains unclear before deciding that one proposal is cheaper.

Use this private worksheet once per proposal. It does not rank firms, calculate a recommended fee or collect your financial information. For every service, record the written evidence, frequency, included or excluded status, cost basis and unresolved question.

Planning and investment design

Cost basis: project, hourly, subscription, asset-based or another disclosed arrangement. Unresolved question: what will be delivered before implementation?

Talk it through

Ask for the all-in cost, not one percentage

Request a cost explanation that separates professional services from product, account and transaction costs. Two proposals can use the same fee label while charging for different work or applying the fee to different balances.

The SEC fees-and-expenses bulletin, published July 23, 2025, explains that fees reduce the amount of money earning returns and can affect portfolio value over time. Use it to understand why cost detail matters, not to assume its hypothetical examples predict your results.

Ask what assets or services are included in the charging basis, how often the charge is assessed and what changes if balances change. Does the proposal include planning? Are cash balances treated differently? Are product expenses separate? What happens if you buy only advice rather than implementation? Ask for dollars where the proposal can support them, with the assumptions written beside the figure.

Do not compare proposals using an invented market average. This page supplies no fee benchmark. Also ask about charges when selling, transferring or ending an engagement, and whether costs differ across recommended products. A lower visible adviser fee does not establish a lower total, and a higher total does not prove better service.

Define monitoring, reporting and rebalancing

Ask what will be reviewed, when you will hear about it and what events trigger another discussion. An initial portfolio is not the same service as an ongoing process.

Investor.gov's allocation guidance explains that changing investment values can move a portfolio away from its intended allocation and that rebalancing brings it back toward that allocation. Ask how the proposed service handles this, including cash contributions or withdrawals, transaction costs and tax questions. Do not assume the guide requires your portfolio to trade at a particular interval.

Request a reporting sample with private information removed. Can you distinguish contributions and withdrawals from investment performance? Does it explain the reporting period, fees and the comparison used? Ask whether results are presented before or after relevant fees and whether outside accounts are included. A chart labeled “performance” needs definitions before it supports a judgment.

Agree on what you must tell the adviser: changing goals, employment, spending, account restrictions or a need for cash. Ask whether the adviser proactively requests updates or expects you to initiate them. Monitoring cannot account for a household change that the responsible person never learns about.

Verify the professional and understand conflicts

Check the firm and individual through official sources, then compare the disclosures with the actual proposal. Registration and background checks are necessary diligence, not an endorsement or guarantee of expertise or results.

FINRA's registration guidance explains checking investment professionals and points readers to relevant resources. Investor.gov's adviser guidance recommends checking the person and firm, services, costs, conflicts and disciplinary history. Do not confuse a broker record with proof of an investment-adviser service; confirm the capacity in which the professional would work with you.

Ask how compensation affects recommendations. Could affiliated products, referral payments, asset transfers or a choice of account produce different compensation? Ask the person to explain the conflict and how it is handled. A fee-only, fiduciary or specialist label is not a reason to skip the details of the engagement.

Read applicable disclosures rather than relying on a conversation alone. If the proposal describes something the documents do not cover, ask for clarification before signing. Experience with someone of a similar age or occupation does not establish that the same recommendations fit your household.

Check custody and compare statements

Identify where assets would be held and how you would independently verify the account. Do not treat an adviser's dashboard or report as the only record of what you own.

The SEC custody bulletin, dated March 4, 2013, explains custody arrangements for SEC-registered advisers and encourages comparison of adviser information with statements from the custodian. This page uses its investor-diligence guidance, not a complete current legal analysis of custody-rule exceptions or every professional's obligations.

Ask for the custodian's identity, legitimate contact route and how statements reach you. Review account ownership, permissions and fee deductions through the relevant documents. If something differs between records, ask both parties to explain it using independently obtained contact details. A familiar logo or polished report is not proof that an unexplained difference is harmless.

Custody safeguards do not prevent market losses or eliminate the need to monitor records. Ask who handles an account-access issue and who can answer a transaction question. Keep your credentials private and establish access through the actual firm's authorized process.

Test the scope with a fictional situation

Imagine a fictional investor who wants help with several accounts but wishes to retain approval of trades. The useful comparison is whether each proposal explains advice, implementation, outside-account review and reporting, not which sample portfolio looks most exciting.

One proposed engagement might offer a planning project; another might offer ongoing management with different authority. Ask what the investor would receive, what they must do themselves and what costs apply. Neither label establishes that it is the right choice. This is a selection exercise, not a client result or an endorsement of either service.

If the professional recommends moving every account immediately, ask how that recommendation follows from the written assignment and how compensation changes. If monitoring is promised, ask for the process and sample reporting. If a tax question appears, ask who reviews it and whether that work is included. The exercise should reveal missing terms rather than produce a guaranteed return.

Bring questions before sharing private records

Prepare a list of accounts, goals and unresolved questions, then agree on secure document sharing after verifying the professional and scope. Do not put account numbers, passwords, statements or detailed financial records into a public inquiry form.

Ask: What decisions will you own? What authority would I grant? What remains my responsibility? Who holds assets? What are all the costs? What gets monitored? How do we handle tax questions and restrictions? How do I verify reporting? What happens if I end the engagement?

Keep the worksheet and documents for your own comparison. The next useful step is clarifying the proposal, not copying a portfolio or treating an age-based calculator result as advice. The educational calculator collection can explain simple model inputs, but no calculator establishes the right investment-management engagement for you.

Conclusion: choose a clear engagement, not a portfolio illustration

Choose investment-management help by the work, authority, custody, full costs and review process in the written proposal. A sample portfolio or familiar fee percentage does not establish what you will receive or what remains your responsibility.

Use the scope-and-cost worksheet privately, mark missing evidence and ask candidates to resolve it before you sign or transfer assets. No allocation, performance outcome or professional fit is guaranteed by this guide.

Common questions

What should an investment-management agreement explain?

It should make the proposed services, exclusions, authority, monitoring and costs understandable. Ask who does the work, which accounts are covered, what remains your responsibility and how the engagement can end. Review the agreement and applicable disclosures rather than relying on a portfolio illustration.

Is advice-only the same as discretionary management?

No. Ask whether the professional recommends actions for you to approve or can trade within agreed authority. Confirm permissions, restrictions and implementation responsibilities in the actual documents. Neither arrangement is recommended for every reader by this guide.

Does diversification guarantee that I will avoid losses?

No. Investor.gov describes diversification as spreading investments to reduce risk, not eliminate every loss. Ask how allocation and underlying exposures connect to your goals and time horizon. Several holdings or accounts do not by themselves establish suitable diversification.

Is the adviser's percentage fee the whole cost?

Not necessarily. Ask about planning charges, investment-product expenses, custody or account costs, transactions and any transfer or exit charges. Compare written proposals for the same work and charging basis. This guide supplies no market fee benchmark.

How can I check where my investments are held?

Ask for the custodian's identity, account documents, legitimate contact route and statement arrangements. The SEC custody bulletin encourages comparing adviser information with custodian records. Clarify discrepancies independently; a dashboard alone is not a complete check or protection against losses.

Does checking registration prove an adviser is right for me?

No. Official checks help establish registration and background, not an endorsement or guarantee of expertise or results. Confirm the capacity in which the person would work, then examine written scope, relevant experience, costs and conflicts.
